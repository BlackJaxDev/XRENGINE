# Full-body calibration integration investigation

## Configuration and evidence

Validation uses the named `vr-fullbody` isolated editor, OpenGL, the Default render pipeline, an imported humanoid, explicit synthetic headset/controller poses, and eight synthetic body trackers. The devices have fixed identities and arbitrary mount rotations. The player standing-height setting is 1.75 m during checks and is restored afterward. No physical headset or tracker result is implied.

Disposable captures, runtime snapshots, build logs, and the regression result are under `Build/_AgentValidation/20260926-190000-vr-fullbody/`. The [implementation record](../../progress/avatar/openxr-full-body-calibration-spectator-implementation.md) tracks the current validation status.

## Eye position and canonical stance

The first live capture measured an eye-to-head vertical offset of 1.282979 m. The eye geometry had been measured in a world basis contaminated by avatar translation. Measuring bind geometry in humanoid-root coordinates corrected that offset to 0.038671494 m, or 0.051738456 m after the entered height was applied. Measurement does not use an animated or scaled world-space denominator.

The avatar's anatomical left side is positive local X. A fixed negative-X assumption crossed its arms during the preview. Canonical arm directions now derive from the bind shoulder separation. A rotated player root explains why left and right limbs can separate primarily along world Z; that is not a binding failure.

The imported avatar faces positive bind Z while the tracked headset uses negative Z as forward. The canonical root now applies the bind-body-to-engine correction before headset yaw, and the measured eye displacement and bind head/wrist rotations use the same basis. In the rebuilt synthetic session, headset yaw was 223.77 degrees and avatar root yaw stayed at 43.77 degrees across repeated stationary reads: the expected 180-degree facing correction, with no observed drift. The head bone and target rotations matched; steady wrist bone and target rotations also matched up to the equivalent quaternion sign.

## Inverted pelvis and legs

The captured hips target was at world Y=2.073632 m and the head target near Y=2.67486 m. The faulty solve repeatedly moved the hips to Y=2.8823 m and lifted the feet near the head. Targets themselves remained correct.

Isolation ruled out optional chest, upper-arm, knee, and foot sources, animator activity, and target render matrices. Disabling the hips constraint removed the inversion. Storing the displayed canonical pose as the solver's reset baseline did not resolve the fault. Solver size is not a replacement for transform scale: virtual bone lengths are read from current world-space positions.

The trigonometric bend calculation combined a look rotation whose positive Z points toward the target with a negative axial component. It therefore aimed the first segment away from the target. It also read adjacent-bone lengths when solving across skipped spine joints. The corrected calculation uses the actual two segment lengths and a direct axial/perpendicular construction, with a stable fallback for a degenerate bend plane.

After rebuilding, eight live hips reads matched the target within approximately 0.000001 m. The head and toes also reached their targets; separate MCP property reads can sample different stages of the animation update and are not an atomic bone-pose snapshot. Turning the chest tracker by 20 degrees rotated the chest by approximately 18.3 degrees. In the final normalized-basis session, steady head, hips, toes, and knee positions were within approximately 2 mm of their targets. A 10 cm left-foot tracker lift moved the toe target and toe bone by 10 cm while the head stayed fixed.

## Optional tracker and session checks

The original synthetic left-controller position placed the hand target about 0.601 m from the upper-arm origin, beyond the combined 0.557 m upper-arm and forearm length. The elbow could not follow an independent tracker freely while preserving that wrist constraint. Moving the controller 20 cm inward and 20 cm up made the hand reachable. A subsequent 12 cm upper-arm tracker lift raised the elbow 4.85 cm; five consecutive samples were stable. A 10 cm local-Z knee tracker move shifted its world goal approximately 6.9 cm in X and 7.2 cm in Z; the knee moved in the same directions by approximately 4.2 cm and 4.9 cm. The corrected knee solver derives the bend plane from the projected tracker goal and interpolates around the limb axis, including opposite bend directions without a zero normal. The controller and tracker poses were restored after these checks. Numeric samples are in `Build/_AgentValidation/20260926-190000-vr-fullbody/reports/core-final-live.json`.

Repeated live captures with zero, three (hips and both feet), and eight valid optional trackers all completed, exercising three-, six-, and eleven-point calibration. All eleven target transforms remained non-null and kept the same identities through each capture. A same-process restore with one bound knee tracker unavailable retained all eleven target identities, set that knee's solver weight to zero, and returned its weight to one when the tracker resumed. All eight synthetic validity flags were restored. These checks validate target ownership and source recovery in the synthetic session; physical device reconnection remains unverified.

A later room-scale check displaced the synthetic headset and hips tracker together by 0.15 m in playspace-local X while retaining their arbitrary tracker mount offset. The playspace root shifted by 0.15 m in world X; the HMD and hips source stayed at their prior world positions after recentering. Head and hips targets and solved bones remained aligned without a doubled offset across five settled reads. Restoring both device matrices returned the playspace and targets to baseline. Samples are in `Build/_AgentValidation/20260926-190000-vr-fullbody/reports/core-roomscale-live.json`. The player root did not move in this synthetic world: its active PhysX character controller reported falling, zero effective velocity, and no horizontal displacement. The input accumulator returned to zero, but these observations do not establish completed physics locomotion. A running physics-world check is still needed for player-root movement.

In the later physics-enabled editor session, the calibrated player reported `MovementMode=Walking` with an upward ground normal. Capture completed with all eleven solver target fields populated. This confirms the grounded state and target ownership in that synthetic run; independent player-root locomotion still needs its own motion observation.

## Spectator output

An unpossessed camera screenshot can silently resolve to the editor viewport. Camera ownership and the active viewport must therefore be checked before interpreting an image. The synthetic pawn is explicitly possessed for spectator checks.

Advanced offscreen capture failed during bindless texture preparation with a pending sampling-parameter transition. The spectator uses the existing Default GPU offscreen path with completion fences and consumer leases. Keeping a live first-person desktop viewport in texture mode preserves the window callbacks which schedule capture.

The completion serial initially advanced while a readback of the published target was entirely zero. Both desktop and private capture views required an initial unculled draw to establish imported skinned bounds. A bounded warm-up now restores frustum culling after those first frames. The final 1920 × 1080 completed texture contained the avatar and environment with finite HDR samples (RGB maximum 1.9570, mean 0.5783), and both private slots resumed culling. The scene-capture policy excluded capsule, axes, and debug labels. Its background alpha is mostly zero; opaque video consumers must force opaque alpha.

An earlier Play screenshot lost the avatar with spectator frustum culling enabled and recovered it when culling was disabled. The later binary was checked at one frozen spectator pose: culling enabled and disabled both rendered the full avatar and debug geometry, with 118 viewport commands and 110 opaque mesh commands in each view. The paired captures are `mcp-captures/Screenshot_20260926_204242_950_f7864fcfbdaa488883bf466e2dfa6e08.png` and `mcp-captures/Screenshot_20260926_204258_209_d3fd6c374f5d40339699d595e3f24988.png` under the task evidence root. A diagnostic run logged `bvh-node` rejections across scene views, but those entries were not correlated to the active spectator view and do not establish a current avatar-culling defect. Frustum culling and spectator follow were restored after the comparison.

Viewed desktop captures at 3 m and 5 m and the offscreen image share bright straight arms above darker bent arms. Live isolation showed that deactivating `Body` removes the dark posed body while the bright arms remain; deactivating the imported `Armature` clothing group removes the bright arms while preserving the posed body. This is not a spectator-only rendering error. The source FBX contains 72 mesh nodes: 34 are skinned to the selected `Armature`, 17 to `Armature.Yuno`, 15 to `Armature.Lumi`, three to `Armature.Milltina`, and three are unskinned. For example, `Body` is bound to `Armature/Hips`, while `Acc - Sleeves`, `C - Hoodie`, and `C - TShirt` bind to `Armature.Yuno` and `C_Arm_Sleeves` binds to `Armature.Lumi`. The selected humanoid solver drives only its own rig, so the foreign-rig garments retain their straight bind pose. The FBX mesh nodes have no authored visibility values; importer loss of authored mesh visibility was ruled out for this asset.

Temporarily hiding all 35 foreign-rig meshes still left one bright straight arm. With that selection held, disabling `Shirt` alone removed the bright arm, while disabling `ACC Arm Sleeves` did not. `Shirt` (`Mesh.008`) is bound to 18 bones also used by `Body` (`Mesh.050`); all 2,725 of its control points have positive bone weights, ruling out the importer's rigid fallback for unweighted vertices. Outer garment vertices have predominantly arm and shoulder weights, so missing arm weights are also ruled out. The authored inverse-bind matrices differ from `Body` by a uniform 0.0254 scale in their rotation/scale elements, while their translation elements match. The garment control points are in a different source scale (approximately 12 units wide versus the body's 1.3), and both skins provide authored transforms. The importer retains each mesh's own bind matrix; replacing the garment bind data with the body's would therefore be unjustified. A live comparison of the imported garment's GPU bone palette and source-space deformed positions at a held arm pose is the next narrow check. All temporary mesh deactivations were restored. Automatic production hiding of foreign rigs or `Shirt` could discard intentional attachments; no asset-specific visibility policy was added. Output publication is validated, but complete-avatar visual acceptance remains open.

## Regression boundary

The former explicit persistence regression initially read six null solver fields immediately after capture. Publication into the humanoid store was not synchronizing the solver fields until the next update; capture now synchronizes on success. With test-edit clearance, the regression now runs normally and checks eleven stable avatar-owned targets through solver updates. Focused calibration, assignment, and input-dispatch tests passed 16/16 in `Build/_AgentValidation/20260926-190000-vr-fullbody/reports/vr-core-tests-final.trx`. The assignment checks cover all 40,320 permutations of eight body trackers with distant extras; capture checks cover three, six, and eleven points, tilt refusal with yaw acceptance, failed-capture state preservation, same-session offset restoration, and rotated-mount/scaled-avatar composition.

The scaled-avatar capture test exposed a separate 1.28-times target translation error when the target root had just been created. Its parent inverse matrix had not yet incorporated avatar scale. Recalculating dirty ancestors before converting a world target pose to target-local space removed the error; the targeted math test and complete focused filter passed afterward.

A broader animation, input, and VR-rendering selection yielded 221 passed and 62 failed (`reports/vr-broad-tests.trx`). Failures cluster in general humanoid pose/mapping, animation import/clip behavior, and rendering source-contract assertions. A representative humanoid neutral-preset failure and an OpenXR source-contract failure both reproduce when run alone (`reports/vr-isolated-humanoid.trx`, `reports/vr-isolated-openxr-contract.trx`), excluding this calibration fixture's static state as their cause. The broad failures have not been fixed or established as regressions from this work.

The action-pose source contract did intersect this change: it expected a two-line success-only sync, whereas the current path invalidates pose and tracker snapshots on sync failure and publishes a fresh frame/sample timestamp after success. The revised assertion checks both paths. A separate deterministic check validates every suggested path in the five advertised controller profiles against specification component lists; it caught unsupported Vive, Touch, and WMR paths that had caused `xrSuggestInteractionProfileBindings` to fail with `XR_ERROR_PATH_UNSUPPORTED` during physical OpenXR startup. The corrected tables and focused calibration, movement, input, and profile selection passed 19/19 (`reports/vr-profile-targeted.trx`). Repeating the same broad class selection after these changes yielded 224 passed and 61 failed (`reports/vr-broad-tests-final.trx`).

Three other timing-pipeline source assertions were checked against committed source before changing them: the frame-timing assertion expects `EndFrameWithTiming(in frameEndInfo)` without the existing `ref` argument; the pacing assertion expects at least four literal submit-signal calls where committed source has two; and the desktop-camera assertion searches for a nullable bootstrap `CreateDesktopCamera` signature where committed source has a nonnullable return. Those are stale assertions about unchanged behavior, separate from the revised action-pose contract. They remain in the broad failure count; this investigation did not weaken them.

Physical OpenXR tracking/profile alignment, controller gestures, repeated editor toggles, Vulkan output, and performance remain hardware acceptance work. They are not established by synthetic scene evidence or the single SteamVR editor borrow/restore cycle above.

The render-world viewport reads a stable span of that array, with no collection copy on each visibility pass. The consolidated editor build completed with zero warnings/errors.
